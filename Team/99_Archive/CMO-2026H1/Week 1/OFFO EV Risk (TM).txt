OFFO EV Risk™
Language Guardrails (Internal Only)

Purpose
Ensure every word we use compounds trust, avoids category confusion, and reinforces our position as decision intelligence, not diagnostics or hype.

This document is non-negotiable for marketing, product copy, sales conversations, and founder posts.

1️⃣ One-Sentence Positioning (Canonical)

OFFO EV Risk™ provides pre-purchase decision intelligence for used EV buyers by explaining what’s missing, why it matters, and how confident you should be before buying.

If a sentence cannot be traced back to this, it does not belong.

2️⃣ Phrases We WILL Use (Approved Language)

These phrases reinforce judgment, clarity, and trust.

Decision intelligence

Signals judgment, not raw data

Differentiates from diagnostics and reports

Contextual risk

Reinforces that battery risk is not binary

Explains nuance without fear

Confidence improvement

Shifts the user’s goal from “perfect data” to “better decisions”

Anchors conversion without pressure

Optional supporting phrases (use sparingly):

“What’s missing — and why it matters”

“Second-opinion clarity”

“Pre-purchase insight”

3️⃣ Phrases We Will NEVER Use (Hard Bans)

These words destroy trust, confuse the category, or invite legal/expectation risk.

❌ Battery diagnostics

Implies hardware testing or inspection

Creates false expectations and legal exposure

❌ AI-powered / AI-driven analysis

Sounds like hype

Reduces credibility in a high-stakes decision

❌ Battery health score

Over-simplifies a complex reality

Positions us as definitive when we are contextual

❌ Inspection / Inspection replacement

We do not replace inspections

We exist before inspections

❌ Dealer verification / Dealer fraud language

Adversarial framing kills future partnerships

We explain gaps; we don’t accuse

If any of these appear in copy, UI, or posts → rewrite immediately.

4️⃣ Tone Rules (Implicit but Critical)

Explain, don’t alarm

Clarify, don’t conclude

Invite judgment, don’t assert authority

Sound like a calm expert friend — not a platform

Bad tone:

“This EV could have serious hidden battery issues.”

Correct tone:

“Here’s what the listing doesn’t tell you — and why buyers often overlook it.”

5️⃣ Quick Self-Check (Before Anything Goes Live)

Ask these three questions:

Does this help the buyer feel more oriented, not more afraid?

Does this explain why uncertainty exists, not pretend we removed it?

Does this sound like judgment, not automation?

If any answer is “no” → revise.

6️⃣ Internal Reminder (CMO Note)

We are not trying to sound impressive.
We are trying to sound reliable.

Trust is our moat.
Language is how we build it.

What you SHOULD write now (safe topics)

These are translation posts — they reflect what the community already said.

✅ Category 1: Pattern Naming (Best)

Examples:

“Why EV regret usually isn’t about range”

“Predictability vs availability: what actually reduces EV friction”

“Why EV ownership feels harder when routines change”

Tone:

Observational

Neutral

No recommendations

✅ Category 2: Vocabulary Builders

Posts that give people language:

“What EV owners mean when they say ‘this mostly works, except…’”

“The hidden mental load of EV ownership”

“Why execution-time failures matter more than specs”

These posts often get quoted, not clicked — which is ideal.

✅ Category 3: Experience Synthesis

Summaries of what you’ve seen (without calling it research):

“What apartment EV owners consistently mention”

“Common friction patterns after switching brands”

“What surprises people after the first 3–6 months”

Frame as:

“Based on recurring community discussions…”

Never as:

“Our analysis shows…”

What a GOOD blog post looks like (structure)

Length: 600–1,000 words
Structure:

One clear framing sentence

3–4 patterns (not tips)

Neutral examples (no anecdotes with brands)

A single closing reflection question

Example closing line:

“Whether this works well or poorly usually has less to do with the vehicle and more to do with how stable your routine really is.”

No CTA. No signup.

Where blogs can be shared (selectively)
Allowed:

Linked privately when someone asks

Referenced in comments only after trust is established

Used as internal onboarding material

Linked from your site for cold traffic (X/MSN)

Not allowed (yet):

Reddit top-level posts

Crossposts

“I wrote about this” replies

SEO pushes

The timing rule (lock this in)

Write now.
Share only when someone asks a question the post already answers.

If the blog exists before the question appears, you’re ahead.
If you share it before permission, you’re selling.

Strategic upside (why this is smart now)

Prepares you for scale without changing tone

Gives engineers/product shared language

Makes future monetization feel inevitable, not forced

Protects you from having to “explain again” later

Bottom line

Yes — start writing.
No — do not start “blogging.”

Treat posts as:

Quiet documentation of hard-earned insight.

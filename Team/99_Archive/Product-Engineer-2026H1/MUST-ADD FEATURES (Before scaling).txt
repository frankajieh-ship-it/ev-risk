MUST-ADD FEATURES (Before scaling traffic)

These address real confusion repeatedly expressed by users.

1. “History × Routine Interaction” Signal (Top-Level Addition)

Problem users have

They can check history (Carfax, DVLA, recalls)

They can check battery health

They cannot tell how that history will feel in their daily life

Feature update
Add a section to every report:

“How this car’s history interacts with your routine”

Example output:

“Frequent fast charging + no home charging = higher routine friction”

“Cold-climate history + long daily miles = reduced buffer in winter”

“Short-trip urban history + predictable home charging = low stress”

This is not new data — it’s interpretation.

Why this matters
This is the exact gap between:

Car Owl / Carfax (what happened)

Battery SoH (what’s left)

OFFO (what it will feel like)

2. Battery Health Contextualization (Absolute → Relative)

Problem users have

“92% battery health — is that good or bad?”

They don’t need a number. They need context.

Feature update
Whenever battery health is shown, anchor it:

“92% after 3 years = typical / slightly above average”

“88% after 2 years = faster than expected decline”

“94% after 5 years = unusually strong”

Add a simple line:

Compared to similar vehicles of this age and usage pattern.

Why
This instantly resolves:

runaway vs normal degradation

panic around numbers without benchmarks

3. “What Sellers Don’t Disclose” Checklist (Micro-Section)

Problem users have
They don’t know what questions separate legit sellers from sketchy ones.

Feature update
Add a collapsible section:

“Commonly missing or avoided disclosures”

Charging habits (DCFC vs home)

Climate exposure

Software-limited range vs degradation

Recall status actually resolved vs “eligible”

This directly answers the Reddit question:

“What questions do I ask that matter?”

⚠️ SHOULD-ADD FEATURES (Next iteration)

These improve clarity and defensibility but aren’t blockers.

4. EV-Specific History Signals (Non-Mechanical)

Car Owl correctly points out:

EVs aren’t low-risk just because they have fewer moving parts.

You can extend that insight without duplicating Carfax.

Feature update
Introduce EV-specific history flags:

Long idle periods at low SOC

Repeated deep discharge patterns (if inferable)

High DCFC dependency

Ownership churn correlated with charging access changes

Even probabilistic language here is valuable.

5. “Confidence Drivers” Transparency Panel

Users trust systems that explain themselves.

Feature update
Add a small box:
“Why we’re confident (or not)”

Data completeness

History clarity

Routine predictability

This already exists partially — formalize it.

❌ FEATURES YOU SHOULD NOT ADD (Yet)

Important restraint here.

❌ Don’t add full legal history checks

That’s Car Owl / Carfax territory.

Your value is interpretation, not raw records.

❌ Don’t add “Buy / Don’t Buy” labels

Reddit will turn on you instantly.

Stick with:

Fit

Friction

Confidence

Tradeoffs

❌ Don’t add generic reliability scores

They collapse nuance and create false certainty.

🔑 META FEATURE (Most Important Insight)
6. Explicitly Frame EVs as “Stored Histories, Not Just Assets”

This isn’t UI — it’s product philosophy embedded in copy.

Add language like:

“Used EVs aren’t just vehicles — they’re usage histories.”

“You’re not buying miles; you’re buying patterns.”

This aligns perfectly with:

The Car Owl post

Reddit anxiety

Your positioning

More data sources

More questions

More scoring

You do need:

Clearer interpretation layers

Stronger context around battery health

Explicit explanation of why history matters differently for EVs

A bridge between what happened and how it will feel

Smarter than Carfax

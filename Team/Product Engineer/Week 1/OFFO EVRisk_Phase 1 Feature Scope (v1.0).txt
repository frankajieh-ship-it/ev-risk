OFFO — Phase 1 Feature Scope Definition (v1.0)

Purpose
Phase 1 exists to explain behavioral fit and mental overhead using information we already have or can ask for lightly.
It is not a scoring phase. It is not a recommendation phase. It is not a shopping phase.

Phase 1 answers one question:

“Does this EV still fit my routine — and why or why not?”

1️⃣ Inputs (What Phase 1 Is Allowed to Use)

Phase 1 may only use inputs that meet both conditions:

Do not materially increase user burden

Do not require new infrastructure, APIs, or inference systems

A. Context Inputs (Lightweight, Explicit)

Reason for re-checking:

Moved home

Charging situation changed

Commute changed

Schedule changed

Just re-checking

These inputs exist to frame interpretation, not to compute outcomes.

B. Charging Context (Self-Reported, Simple)

Primary charging access:

Home (dedicated)

Apartment / shared

Public L2

Public DC fast charging

Mixed

Perceived reliability:

Usually available

Sometimes available

Unpredictable

Weekly charging frequency:

1–2 times

3–4 times

5+ times

These inputs capture perceived predictability, not objective infrastructure quality.

C. Vehicle & Listing Data (Already Available)

Presence or absence of battery health data

Known recalls

Warranty status (known / unknown)

Vehicle class (sedan, SUV, truck)

Range class (standard / extended)

Phase 1 treats missing data as first-class information, not errors.

2️⃣ Outputs (What Phase 1 Produces)

Phase 1 outputs are language-based explanations only.
They are meant to reduce mental overhead, not resolve decisions.

A. Behavioral Explanations

Examples:

“Charging may feel mentally demanding because availability is unpredictable.”

“Battery uncertainty amplifies planning effort when charging isn’t routine.”

“This setup works best when charging is a background habit, not a recurring task.”

Outputs describe why something feels stressful, not whether it is “good” or “bad.”

B. Heuristic Labels (Non-Numeric)

Phase 1 may use descriptive categories, such as:

Low routine friction

Moderate routine friction

High routine friction

Predictable charging

Fragile charging setup

Battery uncertainty present

These are labels, not scores.

C. Failure Mode Visibility

Phase 1 must explicitly surface when common mismatch patterns apply, including:

Apartment charging with unpredictable access

Reliance on DC fast charging as primary fuel

Edge-case usage dominating mental load

Battery uncertainty combined with cold climate or long trips

Failure modes are named calmly, without urgency or alarm.

D. Decision Confidence (Explained, Not Scored)

Phase 1 may explain confidence as:

What is known

What is unknown

What would reduce uncertainty

Example:

“Decision confidence is limited by missing battery health data. With that information, uncertainty around long-term planning would be reduced.”

Confidence is never a judgment of vehicle quality.

3️⃣ What Charging Predictability Means (Conceptual Only)

Charging Predictability refers to:

How reliably charging fits into a user’s normal routine without requiring active planning or repeated adjustments.

Conceptually, charging is predictable when:

Access is usually available at expected times

Charging can be treated as a background habit

Backup options exist within reasonable distance

Missed charging events do not create cascading stress

Phase 1 does not calculate predictability.
It explains conditions that increase or decrease it.

4️⃣ What Routine Fit Means (Conceptual Only)

Routine Fit refers to:

How well EV ownership aligns with a user’s daily and weekly life without creating ongoing cognitive load.

Conceptually, routine fit is strong when:

Charging aligns with existing habits

Energy needs are predictable week to week

Edge cases are rare or mentally discounted

Planning is front-loaded, not continuous

Phase 1 does not judge fit.
It helps users recognize fit or mismatch themselves.

5️⃣ Explicit Prohibitions (Non-Negotiable)

Phase 1 must not include:

Numeric scores (predictability, fit, anxiety, etc.)

Rankings or grades

Vehicle recommendations

Trade-in or switching prompts

Marketplace links

EV vs ICE comparisons

Spec optimization language

Charger counts or map-based claims

Any proposal including these is out of Phase 1 scope and requires CEO approval.

6️⃣ Phase Discipline Statement (Locked)

Phase 1 explains behavior.
It does not decide for the user.

Scoring, optimization, and recommendations are future phases and must not leak into Phase 1 through wording, visuals, or implication.

7️⃣ Final Validation Question (Required)

Before considering Phase 1 complete, we must be able to say:

“A user can now understand why their EV feels easy or stressful — without being told what to buy or do.”

If this is not true, Phase 1 has overreached or under-explained.

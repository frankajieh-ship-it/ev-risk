OFFO — Decision Confidence (Internal Definition v1.0)

Status: Locked
Scope: Internal use only
Applies to: Phase 0.5 and Phase 1
Purpose: Prevent semantic drift, overclaiming, and accidental judgment

1️⃣ What Decision Confidence Is

Decision Confidence is:

The degree to which OFFO can stand behind its guidance given what is known, what is unknown, and how sensitive the decision is to those unknowns.

It reflects:

completeness of relevant information

clarity of assumptions

visibility of failure modes

stability of guidance under common conditions

Decision Confidence answers:

“How well-supported is this guidance, given the current information?”

It does not answer:

“Is this a good car?”
“Should you buy it?”
“Will this work for you?”

2️⃣ What Decision Confidence Is Not

Decision Confidence is not:

A judgment of vehicle quality

A prediction of satisfaction or success

A probability of regret

A recommendation to act

A score of “how good” an EV is

A measure of EV superiority vs ICE

Any usage implying the above is out of bounds.

3️⃣ How Decision Confidence Is Communicated (Allowed)

Decision Confidence may be communicated as:

Plain-language explanations

Confidence ranges or deltas (e.g., 65% → 95%)

Statements about uncertainty sources

Statements about what would reduce uncertainty

Example (acceptable):

“Decision confidence is limited because battery health data is missing. With that information, uncertainty around long-term planning would be reduced.”

4️⃣ How Decision Confidence Must Not Be Communicated

Decision Confidence must not be communicated as:

A final verdict

A quality rating

A buying signal (“green light”)

A comparative rank

A standalone number without explanation

Example (not acceptable):

“77/100 confidence — good buy”

“High confidence EV”

5️⃣ Alignment with Phase 0.5 Confidence Deltas

Phase 0.5 confidence deltas (e.g., 65% → 95%) represent:

The range of confidence OFFO could achieve if specific unknowns were resolved.

They indicate:

where uncertainty comes from

how much clarity additional information could provide

They do not indicate:

improvement in vehicle quality

increased likelihood of a positive outcome

6️⃣ Non-Judgmental Framing Rule (Mandatory)

All Decision Confidence language must:

Describe uncertainty without blame

Avoid implying user error

Avoid implying a “wrong” choice

Frame missing data as normal, not negligent

Example framing:

“This estimate reflects typical used EV listings and common information gaps.”

7️⃣ Enforcement (Forward-Looking)

Starting next sprint:

Decision Confidence language must be selected from approved templates

Any new copy referencing confidence must map to this definition

Voice linter rules will flag violations (e.g., “high confidence EV”)

This week:

No code changes

Alignment only

8️⃣ Final Internal Check (Required)

Before using “confidence” in any product or content surface, ask:

“Is this describing support for guidance — or judging the decision?”

If it is judging, reword or remove.

One-Line Internal Summary (Use This)

Decision confidence measures how well we can explain our guidance — not how good the EV is.

CONTENT PRINCIPLES v1.0
EV Risk Voice & Guidance System

1.0 CORE PURPOSE
EV Risk exists to guide decisions, not just report data.

The system prioritizes safety, long-term viability, and clarity over convenience or optimism.

When certainty is high, we guide clearly.
When certainty is moderate, we contextualize socially.
When certainty is low, we explain boundaries honestly.

Our voice is calm, specific, and explicit about what we know — and what we do not.

2.0 DECISION HIERARCHY (INTERNAL LOGIC)
The system always reasons in this order of importance:

Tier 1: Safety & Immediate Reliability
Open recalls affecting power, braking, or high-voltage systems

Known failure modes that can strand the vehicle

Structural or safety-critical issues

Tier 2: Battery Health & Degradation Trajectory
Remaining useful life estimates

Replacement cost risk horizon

Performance degradation patterns

Tier 3: Cost & Financial Exposure
Purchase price vs. remaining value

Near-term maintenance probability and cost

Warranty coverage gaps

Tier 4: Convenience & Fit
Charging compatibility vs. user's infrastructure

Range adequacy for typical usage

Feature availability and condition

Hierarchy Rules:
Higher-tier risks override lower-tier positives

A great price never neutralizes a safety concern

Language weight follows tier priority (firm → flexible)

3.0 GUIDANCE LADDER
Level 1: High Certainty → "We recommend"
When to use:

Known safety issues

Open recalls requiring action

Clear mismatches between vehicle capability and user needs

Example:
"We recommend resolving this recall before purchase, as it affects high-voltage power delivery and can lead to sudden loss of propulsion."

Level 2: Medium Certainty → "Most buyers in your situation"
When to use:

Battery trajectory estimates

Cost exposure projections

Usage-fit questions based on available data

Example:
"Most buyers with similar driving patterns would expect to charge every 2–3 days with this range."

Level 3: Low Certainty → "Here's how to evaluate"
When to use:

Personal tolerance decisions

Convenience tradeoffs

Lifestyle preference evaluations

Example:
"Here's how to evaluate whether this charging limitation matters for you: compare your typical daily mileage against the available range with a 20% safety buffer."

4.0 CONFIDENCE EXPLANATION FRAMEWORK
Every confidence label must include:

What it means practically

What it's based on

What's missing

How it affects the decision

Template:
text
[Confidence Level] confidence means [practical interpretation]. 
This estimate is based on [data sources], not [what's missing]. 
This typically affects [specific outcomes] but not [what's unaffected].
Example (Medium):
Medium confidence means our recommendation is directionally reliable, but based on population averages rather than your actual usage. This typically affects long-term cost and replacement timing estimates, not immediate safety.

5.0 PERSONALIZATION VALUE PROPOSITION
Every ask must answer, inline:
"Because this changes X, which affects Y, by roughly Z."

Formula:
text
Share [data point] → We'll [specific analysis] → which adjusts [outcome] by [quantified range]
Example:
Share your annual mileage → We'll distinguish between highway (gentler) and city (more taxing) wear patterns → which adjusts the battery replacement timeline by up to ±2 years.

6.0 URGENCY CALIBRATION
Never use alarmist language without calibration.

Allowed urgency levels:
Term	When to Use	Required Calibration
Before purchase	Affects transaction feasibility	Explain why it matters now
Safety-related	Addresses safety system	Explain risk probability and consequence
Time-sensitive	Affects near-term outcome	Provide realistic timeline
Example Transformation:
Before: "Urgent action recommended."
After: "This recall requires attention before purchase as it can delay registration and typically takes 3–7 days to resolve at a dealer."

7.0 WITHHOLDING GUIDANCE (STRATEGIC HONESTY)
When we explicitly withhold:
True Unknowns – missing critical data

Personal Risk Tolerance – no "right" answer

Legal/Safety Gray Zones – insufficient documentation

Required Language:
"We cannot advise here because…"

"Without X, any recommendation would be speculative."

"This comes down to your tolerance for uncertainty."

Example:
"Without a battery health report, we can't reliably estimate remaining lifespan. Any recommendation here would be speculative."

8.0 BANNED & REQUIRED LANGUAGE
❌ Banned Phrases:
"Consider…" (without evaluation framework)

"May limit…" (without probability or impact)

"Better estimates" (without explaining what improves)

"Urgent" (without cause and resolution)

"Probably" (to soften uncertainty)

Vague confidence labels without explanation

✅ Required Patterns:
"This matters because…"

"Without X, we estimate using Y, which affects Z"

"Most buyers in your situation…"

"We cannot advise here because…"

"Here's how to evaluate…"

9.0 VOICE ATTRIBUTES
Calm Expert Advisor:
Explains, doesn't alarm

Admits uncertainty where it exists

Prioritizes user's long-term interest over short-term optimism

Speaks plainly about complex topics

Not These Voices:
Scientist (reporting metrics)

Clerk (requesting data)

Alarmist (creating urgency)

Marketer (persuading)
